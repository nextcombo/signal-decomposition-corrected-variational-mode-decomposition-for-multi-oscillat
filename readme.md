# [Signal Decomposition] Corrected Variational Mode Decomposition for Multi-Oscillation Monitoring Framework [with MATLAB Code]

Thesis: Corrective Variational Mode Decomposition for Multi-Oscillation Detection in Process Control Systems
Abstract: Monitoring oscillatory behavior in industrial control systems is crucial for ensuring process safety and improving production efficiency. However, current decomposition-based monitoring methods struggle to effectively extract and accurately detect multi-oscillations. This issue primarily arises from the sensitivity of existing methods to noise and the intermittent characteristics of multi-oscillations in industrial control systems. Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) has shown potential advantages in processing noisy non-stationary industrial signals. However, improper mode number configuration in VMD can lead to mode mixing, reducing the precision of oscillation extraction. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a Corrective Variational Mode Decomposition (CVMD) method that can automatically adjust the mode number to construct a robust and automated framework for multi-oscillation monitoring. Even in the presence of noise, intermittency, and irregular interference, this framework can efficiently detect oscillatory behavior and quantify the number of oscillations. To validate its effectiveness and practicality, the framework is applied to benchmark industrial datasets and self-constructed industrial datasets, and performance comparisons are made with current advanced oscillation detection methods. The results show that the CVMD framework achieves an accuracy rate of 93.90% in oscillation detection accuracy on the benchmark dataset and 85.37% in oscillation counting accuracy on the self-constructed industrial dataset, achieving similar excellent results.
Introduction: In the digitalization and automation development paradigm advocated by Industry 4.0, the high-performance operation of control systems is crucial (Lang, Chen et al., 2022). However, recent industrial surveys indicate that 41% of industrial control systems exhibit oscillatory phenomena (Dambros et al., 2019). Oscillations can lead to decreased control system performance, increased product scrap rates and raw material consumption, and even pose safety risks (Bounoua et al., 2022). Therefore, as automation continues to be used in industrial process control, the monitoring and suppression of oscillations in the automation process will continue to be an important research direction (Wang et al., 2024).
Oscillation detection is a primary step in oscillation management, playing a crucial role in maintaining controller performance (Wang et al., 2023). For decades, researchers have developed various methods for oscillation detection (Dambros et al., 2021), and Figure 1 briefly outlines typical approaches. In the mid-1990s, Hägglund (1995) achieved oscillation detection by calculating the regularity of integral absolute error (IAE) levels between sufficiently large zero crossing points of continuous control errors, which inspired a series of subsequent studies (Tikkala et al., 2014; Yu et al., 2022; Zakharov et al., 2013). Zakharov et al. (2013) proposed an oscillation detection method based on identifying the maximum and minimum values within each cycle of a time series and evaluating the correlation between segments defined by these maxima. In addition to these time domain methods, robust zero crossing points (RZC) (Tikkala et al., 2014) and data shape techniques (Memarian et al., 2024) have gained widespread attention due to their enhanced noise resistance. Unfortunately, these methods are almost all unable to detect the number of multiple oscillations, significantly hindering root cause analysis (Puli & Huang, 2023).
In the context of multi-oscillation detection, Thornhill et al. (2003) proposed a bandpass filtering method to determine the filter boundaries based on analyzing the power spectral density. However, this algorithm requires manual selection of the filter frequency bands, limiting its portability. To address this issue, Li et al. (2010) introduced an improved discrete cosine transform method that maps data from the time domain to the frequency domain for detection. Additionally, Naghoosi and Huang (2014, 2017) utilized clustering of amplitude-similar autocorrelation function (ACF) peaks and evaluated their regularity to directly identify multi-oscillations. However, these methods still face challenges in specific industrial scenarios such as non-stationarity, noise, sporadic, and irregular oscillations (Puli & Huang, 2023; Wang et al., 2023).
As time-frequency analysis tools evolve, decomposition-based oscillation detection algorithms have gained increasing attention. Srinivas and Rengaswamy (2012) utilized the Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) to decompose process variables (PVs) into a set of Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs), identifying multiple oscillations through detection metrics on these IMFs, where the number of IMFs associated with the oscillations corresponds to the number of oscillation sources in the controller. Additionally, methods based on Local Mean Decomposition (LMD) (Xie et al., 2016), Intrinsic Time Scale Decomposition (ITD) (Xie et al., 2016), Multivariate Empirical Mode Decomposition (MEMD) (Aftab et al., 2018; Lang et al., 2020), and Median Complementary Set EMD (MCEEMD) (Liu et al., 2023) have also been successfully applied to multi-oscillation detection. Although these decomposition techniques can effectively handle non-stationary industrial signals, they often lack robustness in dealing with noise and other intermittent or irregular disturbances. In recent years, Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) (Dewa et al., 2018) and other VMD-based decomposition methods (such as Adaptive VMD (Miao et al., 2019), Fast Adaptive Linear Frequency Modulation Mode Decomposition (FACMD) (Chen et al., 2020), and Self-Tuning VMD (SVMD) (Chen & Chen, 2021)) have emerged as potential methods for multi-oscillation detection due to their solid theoretical foundations and robustness against strong perturbations. However, these methods still face challenges in pre-determining an appropriate number of decomposition modes, as improper mode selection can lead to modal mixing in the decomposed IMFs, significantly reducing the accuracy of oscillation extraction and detection.
To overcome the shortcomings of existing VMD-based methods, this paper proposes a CVMD (Corrective Variational Mode Decomposition) that automatically corrects the number of modes. Building on the original VMD algorithm, the CVMD algorithm constructs and solves an optimization problem aimed at minimizing mode mixing during decomposition under given mode number constraints. By introducing a frequency sparsity measure (FSM) to quantify the degree of mode mixing in the IMFs obtained from VMD decomposition, the optimization problem is constructed. Subsequently, by integrating with mature oscillation detection metrics, a multiple oscillation detection framework based on CVMD is developed. This advanced framework can accurately extract and identify multiple oscillations in control systems even under strong perturbations. Compared to traditional techniques such as EMD, VMD, SVMD, and FACMD, the CVMD-based method demonstrates superior performance and practicality on benchmark industrial datasets and self-constructed industrial datasets.

3.2 Validity analysis of FSM The degree of mode mixing is divided into three categories: mode fusion (mixing different frequency oscillation components into the same IMF), mode splitting (dividing the same frequency oscillation components into different IMFs), and mixed mode mixing (a combination of both). Theoretical analysis shows that when there is no mode mixing, the FSM value is minimal, and in all three cases of mode mixing, the FSM value significantly increases, verifying the effectiveness of FSM in quantifying mode mixing.

Conclusion This paper proposes a multiple-oscillation monitoring framework for process control systems based on the Modified Variational Mode Decomposition (CVMD). This framework, building upon VMD, achieves automatic mode number calibration by constructing and solving an optimization problem that minimizes mode mixing. It effectively suppresses mode mixing and improves the accuracy of oscillation extraction. By integrating CVMD with mature oscillation detection indicators (, and), an integrated framework for multiple-oscillation extraction and detection is formed.
Through the verification with the International Database of Susceptibility (ISDB) and self-built phosphorus plant dataset, this framework demonstrates excellent practicality and effectiveness. On the ISDB dataset, the accuracy rate of oscillation detection reached 93.90%, and the accuracy rate of oscillation quantification reached 85.37%. On the self-built dataset, the detection accuracy reached 91.97%, and the quantification accuracy reached 83.33%, both significantly better than EMD, VMD, SVMD, and FACMD among other comparative methods. This indicates that CVMD is suitable for processing complex industrial signals containing noise, intermittency, and irregular components.
Future research plans to explore data preprocessing techniques to enhance the quality of data in industrial environments, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of oscillation detection algorithms. At the same time, in-depth research on the significance and impact of oscillation intensity in oscillation detection and maintenance is conducted based on high-quality oscillation signals.
